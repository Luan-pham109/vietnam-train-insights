TRAVELLING TO DA NANG BY TRAIN? HERE'S WHAT YOU SHOULD KNOW
Vietnam's train journeys are a great way to see more of the country. You can travel overnight, book a sleeper cabin for the family, or enjoy a scenic ride along the coast.
If Da Nang is on your itinerary, there are two main long-distance routes to consider: Hanoi to Da Nang and Ho Chi Minh City to Da Nang. You can also break up the journey with stops in places such as Ninh Binh, Dong Hoi, Hue, Nha Trang or Quy Nhon.
1. HANOI → DA NANG. Journey time: approximately 15–17 hours, depending on the service. For this long journey, an overnight sleeper is often the most practical choice.
Vietnam Railways (VNR): Regular trains include services such as SE1, SE3, SE5, SE7 and SE19.
Common ticket options and approximate prices: Soft seat: 500,000–600,000 VND; 6-berth sleeper: 610,000–890,000 VND per berth; 4-berth sleeper: 830,000–1,030,000 VND per berth; VIP 2-berth cabin on selected services: around 1.9–2 million VND per berth.
Private tourist carriages: Lotus Express, Laman Express, Livitrans Express / New Livitrans, Violette Express Train. As a rough guide, a 4-berth cabin may cost around US$70–90 per berth, while a VIP 2-berth cabin may cost around US$170–190 for the cabin. Prices vary by operator and departure. Private-carriage prices can be quoted per berth or per cabin. These carriages generally operate as part of a Vietnam Railways train, rather than as completely separate trains.
Where can you stop along the way? Ninh Binh, Dong Hoi (gateway for Phong Nha, road transfer needed), Hue.
2. HO CHI MINH CITY → DA NANG. Journey time: approximately 16.5–19.5 hours. Vietnam Railways operates several trains on this route, including SE2, SE4, SE6, SE8 and SE10. As a rough guide, 6-berth sleeper berths may start around US$41, while 4-berth berths may start around US$56.
Private sleeper options: Hana Train and Charmy Express. These services operate in conjunction with the railway rather than as independent high-speed trains.
What about the SE22 train? SE22 is another service worth checking for the Saigon–Da Nang journey, promoted as a higher-quality option, with sleeper cabins and, on some configurations, special family compartments.
Places to visit on the way: Nha Trang, Quy Nhon (the train generally serves Dieu Tri station rather than the centre of Quy Nhon, so allow time for a transfer into town).
3. A LUXURY COASTAL JOURNEY: THE VIETAGE BY ANANTARA. A luxury rail carriage with comfortable private booths, scenic views and an onboard dining experience. One of its routes runs from Da Nang to Quy Nhon and continues to Nha Trang. For reference, 2026 fares listed for the Da Nang–Quy Nhon journey are around US$475 per person, including service charges and tax.
4. A SHORT BUT BEAUTIFUL TRAIN RIDE: HUE → DA NANG. The 'Connecting Central Heritage' train between Hue and Da Nang, around three hours, crosses the Hai Van Pass, with views of mountains, coastline and Lang Co Bay. Basic tickets have been listed at around 150,000 VND. The service has typically operated two round trips daily.
5. PRACTICAL TIPS FOR TAKING THE TRAIN IN VIETNAM. Book the right cabin for your family. Check exactly what you're paying for: a price shown per berth is not the same as a price for the whole cabin. Bring snacks and water. Pack a small overnight bag. Bring entertainment; internet connections can be unreliable along parts of the route. Expect some movement and noise; earplugs and an eye mask can help. Be especially careful during October and November: heavy rain and flooding can disrupt railway services in Central Vietnam. Compare the whole journey, not just the ticket.
