TRAIN TRAVEL IN VIETNAM: EVERYTHING YOU NEED TO KNOW
Ever dreamed of falling asleep to the gentle rhythm of the rails and waking up to sunrise over Vietnam's lush landscapes?
This guide will walk you through everything you need to know: ticket classes, how to book, and key tips for an unforgettable ride
WHY TAKE THE TRAIN?
Vietnam's rail network runs the length of the country, mostly hugging the coastline - which means insane views: rice paddies, mountains, and stretches of coastline you'll never see from a plane!
It's also a much more relaxed way to travel than a sleeper bus, with more room to move around and a genuine sense of the country passing by outside your window.
THE MAIN ROUTES:
Hanoi ↔ Ho Chi Minh City ('Reunification Express') — roughly 1,700km and around 30-32 hours end to end
Hanoi ↔ Lao Cai (Sapa) — the classic overnight route north
Hanoi ↔ Da Nang / Hue — one of the most scenic legs, along the coast and over the Hai Van Pass
Da Nang ↔ Ho Chi Minh City — a longer haul south
SEAT & CABIN CLASSES
Soft seat (AC) - budget-friendly, fine for day trips or short hops
Hard sleeper (6-berth cabin) - cheapest overnight option
Soft sleeper (4-berth cabin, AC) - the sweet spot for comfort vs. price, most popular with travelers
VIP/deluxe cabins - some operators (like the Livitrans or Chapa Express on the Sapa route) offer boutique-style private cabins
BOOKING TIPS
Book via Vietnam Railways' official site, 12Go, Vexere or a local agency
Reserve popular overnight routes (Hanoi–Sapa especially) several days ahead, more during holidays
Bring snacks - food carts exist but selection is limited
Upper berths are usually cheaper than lower ones in sleeper cabins
Trains run reliably on schedule
If your route has a rail line, it's worth considering over the bus - especially the Hanoi–Sapa overnight or the Hue–Da Nang coastal stretch
