Sleeper Train
If you're considering a sleeper train and you're able to, I'd definitely recommend spending the extra money on a "luxury train." We did not and wish we would have.
We took a sleeper train from Da Nang to Hanoi we paid for "VIP room" (2 bunk beds) but it didn't feel very VIP.
We arrived and our table had a chilli/sugar substance on it.
My bed had a couple grains of dried rice, hair, and hair on my pillow.
I cleaned it off and tried to keep a good attitude. I went looking for the bathrooms and was appalled. One was soaking wet. Toilet, walls, mirror, floor…wet. Had it just been cleaned? Doubtful, because there was still urine in the toilet. There also was not any toilet paper. I found an acceptable toilet 2 carts away.
Employees and riders playing their phones with high volume with doors open. The AC was not consistently on, so rooms became very warm and stuffy making it hard to sleep. I also had to sleep with my foot on our door as it rattled all night. What sent me over the edge was people smoking in between train cars WITH THE DOORS OPEN. I went and closed both doors and opened the window outside our room. An employee later came and closed it.
I've lived in a few countries and have traveled to dozens. I'm fairly adaptable. At bare minimum a dry toilet with toilet paper and no smoking near my children would have been ideal.
Pros: Scenic views; Comfortable temp when AC was on; Storage for luggage in room; Outlets and USB near bed

Luxury train tickets in Vietnam are sold based on coaches or carts. Each one of them is operated by different companies who will arrange the furniture and take care of all facilities, including the toilets. The state-owned usually is a bit more basic, for it is cheaper, but they are quite clean. Luxurious does not always guarantee cleanliness, especially when it is staffed by the not-so-professional resources. It is important to know with whom we order our ticket, the agent or sub-agent, or directly from the government website.
