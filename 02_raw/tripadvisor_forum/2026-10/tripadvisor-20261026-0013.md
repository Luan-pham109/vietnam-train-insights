The Man in Seat 61 address needs a few more letters: https://www.seat61.com/Vietnam.htm; it is a useful site but does not answer David's questions because it says nothing about the HD trains, or, as far as rayza has seen, the left-or-right side question on the Hue-Da Nang section; if going north to south, on SE or HD trains, book the left side for the direct coastal views, which rayza has done; if travelling south to north on an SE train, rayza is not certain - trains from the south turn into Da Nang and the track comes to a terminus/dead end, so to continue north they must reverse, the last carriage in is the first carriage out, and left-side seats become right-side seats, with a 15-minute break between arrival and departure to manage the locomotive change however they do that, so rayza figures a left-side seat booked going north becomes the right-side seaview seat after Da Nang; for an HD train from Da Nang, rayza assumes they have plenty of time to shunt the locomotive and carriages around for it to head out as on the seat booking plans with the seaview seats on the right side; rayza repeats that he is not certain. [c:bee512d1fb]
If David books 5 beds and 4 of the beds are in one room, they can all stay in the one room for the whole trip, no problem; Shell28's family of 4 had just had a trip on the SE2 in one room and it was enjoyable, and in fact they will consider train travel over flying for future trips in Vietnam, for shorter trips; they travelled from Dieu tri (Quy Nhon) to Danang; Shell28 is happy to answer any other questions on the SE train.

[c:a628e2893f]
The train being turned is not an issue for the original poster, who is only travelling between Hue and Da Nang; as David is travelling south, the ocean is on his left, and if he wants a window seat - likely that the window seat will be dirty and not suitable to photograph through - he should book a seat on the left; he should also consider that the local train will most likely have opening windows and also the opportunity to stand between the carriages for photos, if that is his thing; the trip is only about 2.5 hours long, so it is up to him if he really wants/needs a sleeper; also a sleeper will/may only have one window and be positioned on one side of the train with a corridor outside - not really suitable for 5 people wanting to take in the view; he should also take into account that some of the trains are the Reunification Express, travelling between Hanoi and Saigon, and people do book the entire journey. Signed "Greg".
