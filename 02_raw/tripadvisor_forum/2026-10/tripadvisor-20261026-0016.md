Hi everyone, I'm considering taking the overnight train from Hanoi to Sapa and the overnight sleeper bus from Sapa to Ninh Binh to save time on my upcoming trip. (or possibly vice versa.) For those who have done these routes: Was travelling overnight worth the time saved? Which train and bus companies would you recommend? Are there any companies you would avoid? They used Sao Viet bus recently to get to Sapa from Hanoi and could not fault them, but they travelled in the daytime; previous experience of night buses has put them off for ever; either to Sapa or to Ninh Binh will involve some windy mountain roads, a lot of horn beeping and usually some quick braking, therefore it is not relaxing and you do not get much sleep; in the day it may feel like a waste of time, but at least you arrive in a fit state to explore and not in a sleep-deprived mess; trains are OK, no doubt safer, but again night-time travel is not usually great and often you arrive in the early hours (page text reads "easy hours") and cannot check in unless you have also booked the night before; the trains are clunky, people smoke, and the tracks are never straight so you get rocked about; JoRutland would not think of it as saving time or money because it could knock you out for a day as you recover, losing whatever time you have gained; it is also a way to see the scenery, listen to music and podcasts by day and no doubt have a bit of snoozing; the wider beds are better if you are very tall, usually buses with 24 seats, while the 32 ones tend to be the smaller beds; all beds can be sat up so you are not flat unless you want to be; few have toilets, and they stop every few hours in the day; night buses or trains also mean you check out your hotel in the morning, catch them late at night when you are knackered and unshowered from the day, and then arrive long before check-in. "Not my idea of fun."

[c:a6511d30fa]
As for train companies, unless you pay for the high-end ones (and even they can vary) they all seem to be pretty much the same; a four-berth cabin is usually on [wording as displayed], a six can be a pain especially if the empty beds get taken by people who only booked seats; the two berths get booked really quickly; JoRutland has done many four-berth trips and has been OK, and advises just taking water, snacks and wet wipes.

yvwords did the overnight train from Lao Cai to Hanoi, in a 4-berth cabin which two of them had to themselves by bribing the cabin attendant; the trip started with a hair-raising ride in a car from Sapa to Lao Cai in the dark on a very dangerous mountain road with crazy drivers everywhere; if you are in the upper berths in the carriage, it can get very hot; the toilets become unpleasant fairly quickly, given how bumpy the ride is; they did not get a good night's sleep - the track was rough and the train bounced, swayed and bumped, it was noisy, and there were several stops en route, which involved jerky braking, door slamming, noisy departing and arriving passengers, loud departure whistles and bright lights on the stations; on arrival before dawn, they had to carry their luggage up a steep staircase, walk over the tracks and down again to the forecourt, where they were "attacked by a mob of taxi drivers all vying for your custom".

[c:38fd5b29fa]
SimonPedal thinks the trains are more luxurious now but does not know; he has done the trip twice, Hanoi to Lao Cai in the early 2000s and 2010s, and also Saigon to Nha Trang on a similar train; then the carriages were fairly basic but they did the job and got him there; it was cheap and convenient; not like travelling in the UK or EU, but he does not travel all the way to Asia to be like travelling at home.

[c:4ed20ab268]
The overnight train is the classic way to do it and it is part of the experience; book the tourist carriages run by the travel companies, not the standard railway berths - they are cleaner, the bedding is better, and the staff actually help you; a 4-berth soft sleeper is the comfortable choice, while the 6-berth is cheaper but cramped; if you are tall, check the berth length before committing, as some of the older carriages are short; bring snacks and water, as the dining options on board are thin; and set an alarm - the train arrives in Lao Cai very early, around 5:30 to 6am, and the minivans to Sapa leave from right outside the station, so you will be in Sapa by 7:30, in time for breakfast with a mountain view.
